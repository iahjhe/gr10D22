Grade 10 Math

Word Problems Involving Circles

Arc length, sectors, and angles — solved step by step.

Use ▸ Next or the arrow keys. Each worked example reveals one step at a time. Exercise answers stay hidden until you press Show answer.

Key ideas

1. Arc length
degree measure of the arc360 = arc length2πr
2. Area of the circle
A = πr²
3. Area of a sector
Circle area × arc measure360  or, for equal sectors: circle area# of sectors
4. Handy fact
A semicircle is 180°, so its arc is half of the circumference.

Note: these notes use π = 3.15, so we will too.

Tip: a full circle is 360°. Dividing it into equal parts? Divide 360° by the number of parts.

Example 1

The dart board

A dart board has a diameter of 40 cm and is divided into 20 congruent sectors. What is the area of one sector?

a. Measure of each arc
360° ÷ 20 = 18°
b. Radius
40 ÷ 2 = 20 cm
c. Area of the circle
A = πr² = 3.15(20)² = 3.15(400) = 1,260 cm²
d. Area of each sector
1,26020 = 63 cm²

(The book writes m², but the dimensions are in cm.)

One sector = 18°

Example 2

Mr. Celso’s iron arch

Two concentric semicircles; inner diameter 4 m; segments between them are 0.8 m. How much bent iron is used?

Plan
Total bent iron = inner arc + outer arc.
a. Inner arc (r = 4 ÷ 2 = 2)
180360 = L2(3.15)(2) → L = ½ × 12.6 = 6.3 m
b. Outer arc (r = 2 + 0.8 = 2.8)
180360 = L2(3.15)(2.8) → L = ½ × 17.64 = 8.82 m
c. Add them
6.3 + 8.82 = 15.12 m

Extra: if you also count the 12 segments (12 × 0.8 = 9.6 m), the total would be 24.72 m. The book’s answer counts the two arcs only.

Exercise 27 · #1

Clock angle

Find the degree measure of the angle formed by the hands of a clock at 5:00 in the afternoon.

Hint: the clock has 12 numbers around 360°.

360° ÷ 12 = 30° between each number.

From 12 to 5 is 5 numbers: 5 × 30° = 150°

Exercise 27 · #2

Fruit pie

A fruit pie is divided into 3 equal parts. Find the area of each sector if the radius is 6 inches.

Hint: find the whole pie’s area first, then divide by 3.

Central angle: 360° ÷ 3 = 120°

Pie area: 3.15(6)² = 3.15(36) = 113.4 in²

Each sector: 113.4 ÷ 3 = 37.8 in²

Exercise 27 · #3

Garage arch

A semicircle arch of bent iron has a diameter of 3 m. How long should the iron be before bending, to avoid wastage?

Hint: the iron’s length equals the arc length (180°).

r = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m

180360 = L2(3.15)(1.5)

L = ½ × 9.45 = 4.725 m (about 4.73 m)

You did it! 🎉

Dart board sector: 63 cm²

Iron arch (Example 2): 15.12 m

Clock at 5:00: 150°

Pie sector: 37.8 in²

Garage arch: 4.725 m

Remember

  • Equal parts → 360° ÷ parts
  • Arc: degree/360 = L/2πr
  • Sector area = circle area × degree/360
